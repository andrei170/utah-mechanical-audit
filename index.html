Your Booking Page Publishes A Different Phone Number To The Rest Of Your Site.

Prepared for Jose Lopez · Utah Mechanical and Services LLC · 18 September 2026 · AG1 Consulting
2029
the last four digits on your Schedule page · everywhere else it is 6029

You asked how we qualify leads and prove they came from us. The honest answer starts with this: nothing on your site can measure a lead today.

No analytics. No conversion tracking. No contact form anywhere. No tappable phone number. And the one page built to take bookings sends people to a number that is one digit different from yours.

Read from your own served pages, 18 September 2026
Finding 1 — The Wrong Number On The Schedule Page
Homepage, Services, FAQ, service pages: (385) 505-6029 /schedule page: (385) 505-2029 <- one digit different
The page whose whole job is booking work

Somebody who reads your site, decides to call, and clicks "Schedule" gets a number that is not yours. You would never hear about it, because the call simply never arrives. This is a two-minute fix and it is the first thing we would change.

Finding 2 — Nothing On The Site Can Answer Your Question
Google Analytics: none Conversion tracking: none Meta pixel: none Call tracking: none Contact or quote form: none on any page, including /schedule Tappable phone number: none — every number is plain text
You asked exactly the right question

"How do you qualify the leads and prove they came to me" is the question most contractors never ask. The reason nobody can answer it today is that there is no instrument in place. Every enquiry arrives as a call or a message with no record of where it came from.

How we would answer it: a form that asks the qualifying questions before anyone reaches you, a tracked phone number so calls are counted, and a record against every job showing which advert, page or search produced it. That is what turns "we sent you leads" into "here are the twelve jobs, and here is where each one came from".

Finding 3 — On A Phone, There Is Nothing To Tap
Your phone number appears as: plain text What it should be: a tap-to-call link Most HVAC searches happen on a phone, often when something has stopped working.
This is the cheapest repair on the list

Somebody with no heat at 9pm does not copy a number down. They tap it. Right now there is nothing to tap and no form to fill in, so the only people who reach you are the ones determined enough to dial it manually.

Finding 4 — You Name 80 Towns And Have A Page For None Of Them
Pages on your site: 15 Pages built for a city you serve: 0 Your service areas page lists 7 counties and around 80 city names — all as plain text on one page. Comfort Solutions (Utah County): 205 pages published
A list of towns is not the same as a page per town

Somebody searching for HVAC repair in Lehi or Eagle Mountain is shown pages about Lehi and Eagle Mountain. A single page listing eighty towns does not compete with that. The bigger Utah County firms have two hundred pages; you have fifteen.

Finding 5 — What Your Website Is Actually Built On
Platform: Google Sites (Google's free website builder) Page titles: "Home" "About Us" "FAQ" "Heating" <- no service, no city, no business name Meta descriptions: none on any page we checked Business markup: none (no LocalBusiness or FAQ schema) Your FAQ page: 12 real questions and answers — but without the markup that wins the answer box
This is the part to raise with whoever handles your website

Your page titles are the single line Google shows in its results, and yours say "Home" and "FAQ". There are no descriptions, no business markup, and the whole site sits on a free builder. The FAQ content is genuinely good and is the closest thing you have to AEO — but without FAQ markup it cannot be picked up as an answer.

Finding 6 — Your Proof Is On Facebook, Not On Your Site
Facebook: "100% recommend (24 reviews)" On your website: no reviews, ratings or testimonials anywhere Google Ads Transparency Center for your domain: "No ads found"
Twenty-four people recommending you, and none of them on the site

You state "licensed, insured and BBB accredited" and "more than 15 years of hands-on experience". Those, your reviews and your licence number belong on the homepage, because a homeowner choosing between three HVAC firms is looking for exactly those.

What We Would Do, In Order
  • Fix the Schedule page number today. Two minutes.
  • Make every number tappable and put a qualifying form on the site.
  • Install measurement — analytics, call tracking and conversion tracking — so every lead has a source.
  • Put the reviews, the licence and the 15 years on the homepage.
  • Build a page for each city that matters, starting with the ones you actually want work in.
  • Then advertise, reported on cost per booked job, with every lead traced back to its source.
